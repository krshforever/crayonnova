Manifesto

Wax outlives everything.

We started Crayon Nova with one stubborn belief: the further the future goes, the more people will crave things that feel made by hands. Screens got sharper. Feeling got rarer. So we built a crayon line for 2099 that refuses to behave like software.

“A perfect line is a dead line. The wobble is where the human lives.”

Every Crayon Nova stick is cast from neon wax with a holographic core, so a stroke shifts shade with the angle of the light. The wrapper is paper, because paper is honest. The box is cardboard, because cardboard remembers. Nothing about it updates itself at 3am, and that is precisely the point.

We are not nostalgic. Nostalgia looks backward. We are building the material of the next century out of the oldest drawing tool we have — and giving it colors that didn't exist until someone aimed a laser at them.

“If a child in 2099 can't smell the wax, we failed.”

How a crayon is born

Five steps, no shortcuts.

01

Sketch

Every shade starts as a hand-drawn swatch on scrap paper. If it doesn't sing on paper, it doesn't get cast.

02

Wax

Pigments are milled into paraffin with a measured dose of holo-flake. The core is what makes colors shift as you tilt them.

03

Cast

Sticks are poured in low-gravity molds, which is why the tips come out slightly imperfect — and why every stroke stays a little human.

04

Test

Each batch gets drawn with: on cold paper, warm paper, glass, and the sketch pad of a seven-year-old. The seven-year-old's opinion is final.

05

Box

Eighteen to a box. No bonus colors, no gimmicks, no app. Just wax, paper, and the next thing you'll make.

Straight answers

Questions we actually get.

Is Crayon Nova a real product?

No — and yes. The brand, the colors and this site are a design experiment: a futuristic crayon line built as a working piece of the web. The crayons themselves exist as hex codes and as strokes in the Sketch Lab. The wax is still in orbit.

Why does the whole site look hand-drawn?

Because precision is cheap now. Machines draw perfect circles for free; imperfection is the expensive part. Every border here uses irregular blob radii, every icon runs through an SVG turbulence filter that shakes the lines, and there's a paper-grain layer over the entire page.

How does the Roll-a-Crayon thing work?

One CSS variable called --accent drives every button, glow, focus ring and underline. Picking a crayon writes the variable to document.documentElement and saves it to localStorage, so your color follows you across pages and visits. Reset button clears it.

What's actually in the Sketch Lab?

A canvas engine that draws each stroke as several jittered paths with random alpha, plus speckled grain along the path. Stroke width responds to pointer speed — draw fast and thin, slow and thick. Marker mode is a fat translucent stroke; eraser repaints paper. Undo keeps a capped snapshot stack; export serializes the canvas to PNG.

Can I use the hex codes?

Please do. They're on The 18 page — every swatch has a copy button. Credit is optional, enthusiasm is mandatory.
